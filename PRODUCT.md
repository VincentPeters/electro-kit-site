# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Children of roughly 8 to 12, working through the KOSMOS *Easy Elektro Start*
kit at a kitchen or bedroom table. A phone or tablet is propped up next to the
baseplate, screen on, one hand holding a snap connector. Ambient light is
normal room light, sometimes bright daylight.

The job to be done is physical, not informational: build this circuit, switch
it on, understand why it did that. The screen is a bench instrument. It gets
glanced at between actions, at arm's length, often with a thumb as the only
free input device. Parents and teachers read the same pages over a shoulder,
but they are not who the layout is tuned for.

## Product Purpose

An English companion for a kit whose manual ships in German. All 60
experiments, every build plan redrawn as our own SVG against a shared part
registry, so the diagrams on the site use exactly the same visual vocabulary
as the parts in the box.

Success is a child who can go from the page to a working circuit without an
adult translating, and who comes away knowing what the circuit did and why.

## Brand Personality

Workshop-confident. Precise, tactile, engineered.

The site treats a nine-year-old as a real experimenter running real equipment.
It does not perform childishness at them, and it does not hide behind adult
dryness either. Instructions are direct and physical ("clip the strip from C3
to C5"), explanations are curious and plain-spoken.

Three words: **exact, sturdy, unpatronising.**

## Anti-references

- **The primary-colour kids' site.** Cartoon mascots, bubbly rounded
  everything, red/yellow/blue confetti. The reflex answer for anything aimed at
  children, and it would undercut the one thing this site is: accurate.
- **The generic docs template.** System font, soft grey cards, blue links,
  polite whitespace saying nothing. This is what the site looked like before.
- **The blueprint / graph-paper cliché.** Dark navy, cyan drafting lines,
  monospace labels. The obvious "make it look electronics-y" move, and it would
  fight the diagrams instead of framing them.
- **Cream / beige editorial.** Warm near-white paper, display serif, ruled
  columns. Wrong register and the saturated default.

## Design Principles

1. **The circuit is the only colourful thing on a build page.** Blue carries
   current, red uses it, green switches it, black measures it. That code is the
   kit's, printed on the physical parts. Site chrome (nav, links, buttons,
   borders, cards) never borrows those hues, because a child reading a diagram
   must be able to trust that blue means conductor. Colour is loud where there
   is no circuit and silent where there is one.
2. **Design for arm's length and one thumb.** Step text, tap targets and
   diagrams are sized for a propped-up screen seen from across a table, not for
   a laptop at 60cm. If it needs a pinch-zoom to follow while building, it is
   broken.
3. **Say what to do, then what happened.** Every page holds that order and
   makes the seam between the two obvious at a glance. A child mid-build should
   never have to read an explanation to find the next instruction.
4. **Borrow from the object, not from the category.** The hexagonal hole grid,
   the snap studs, the numbers printed on each part, the terminal lettering:
   the visual system comes from the thing in the box. Nothing is decorated to
   look technical.
5. **Exactness is the personality.** Weight, scale and precision carry the
   voice. There is no ornament doing that job.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal conformance target was set for this project. Readability is still
treated as a craft requirement rather than a checklist: body text holds real
contrast against its background, the five chapter colours are always paired
with a number and a name so hue is never the only signal, motion respects
`prefers-reduced-motion`, and experiment pages keep their print styles so a
page can be taken to the table on paper.
